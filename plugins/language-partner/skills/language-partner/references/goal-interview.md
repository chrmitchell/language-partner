# Goal interview (first chat in a language)

Run this when `get_learner_profile` says there is no profile yet. It replaces the first part of the first session. Keep it friendly and short: three or four questions, one per turn, voice-first rules still apply.

## How to ask

- Ask in the target language at a gentle level, using very short sentences. Say up front, in English, that they can answer in English or the target language.
- If they clearly can't follow yet, switch the question to English without fuss.
- Ask one question per turn. Combine where natural so it stays at three or four turns.
- Don't interrogate. React to each answer in a sentence before the next question.

## What to find out

1. Who they are: their name, and how they'd introduce themselves in the target language (some people use a local form of their name).
2. Why they're learning and by when: a trip, family, work, a move, an exam, fun. Any dates.
3. Which situations matter most: for example ordering food, directions, small talk, work meetings, talking with a partner's family.
4. Their current level, in their own words: what they can already say and understand, and what's hard (for example "fast speech loses me").
5. How they like corrections: only big errors, most errors, or explicit corrections; and how much English they want.

If they volunteer more (voice mode or typing, romanisation for a new script, topics to avoid), note it.

## After the interview

1. Write the profile using `references/profile-template.md`, filling in only what they told you. Leave a section's line as "Not known yet" rather than guessing.
2. Save it with `update_learner_profile(language, profile)`.
3. Tell them in one short English sentence that you'll remember this next time and they can ask you to change it any time.
4. Start practising straight away with an easy question tied to their main goal.

If the Flashr tools aren't available, still ask the questions so you can pitch the chat, but don't promise to remember the answers.
